embedConsent

Holds a third-party embed's place until the visitor has allowed it — and skips the asking where they already have.

Every box below is a facade: a locally hosted poster, a notice saying who would receive what, a button that loads it, and a link to the content at the provider for anybody who would rather go there. Nothing is requested from Google, Vimeo or OpenStreetMap until one of the two lanes says it may be.

Your consent platform, played by two buttons

These are not part of the component and not a cookie banner. They write and clear <html data-consent>, which is all the shipped gate lane reads — a real platform writes the same attribute from its own callback, in about five lines. Everything else on this page reacts to it without knowing it exists.

Consent for YouTube, Google Maps and OpenStreetMap:

1 — YouTube, with a poster

The click lane in full. The poster is served from this project — a provider's thumbnail endpoint would be the very request being deferred. Press the button and the player is built with autoplay — which is what its label promises — and granting consent above builds the same player without it.

3 — OpenStreetMap

The same wiring, one attribute different. Whether this one needs consent at all is a project's call — the IP still travels — but there is no advertising business at the other end of it, and that is the cheapest answer to the whole question there is.

4 — No notice, no button

The notice element is here but empty, so the script leaves the button hidden and reports why. This is the one place the component overrules the author: a button that loads third-party content without saying who receives what is not informed consent. The link still works, and granting consent above still fills the box — a platform collected its own consent with its own information.

Open in OpenStreetMap instead

5 — One press, a whole group

A page with six videos on it costs a visitor six presses. data-embedconsent-group lets one press fill every other box naming the same service token — for this page view, storing nothing, exactly as a single press stores nothing. Press either button below and both boxes fill; only the one you pressed starts playing, because nobody asked for the other one. Example 1 above is YouTube as well and stays put: it carries no group attribute, and the default is and remains one press, one box.

Two things to listen for. The buttons are empty in the markup, so the script writes the label — and it says the press reaches further, because a control that describes itself wrongly is the one thing this component will not ship. And the box you press announces once, on behalf of the group, that another embed was loaded too; the box that filled without being asked stays silent, so six boxes never produce six announcements.
