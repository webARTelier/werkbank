slider

A slider an editor places by naming its type — Swiper underneath, a scroll-snap strip without it.

Every slider below is the same markup with a different data-slider-type. Drag them, press the arrows, use the bullets — and Tab into one and press ← / → to see the keyboard control, which is on by default and belongs to whichever slider the focus is in.

The default type: one slide at a time

No data-slider-type at all, which is what an editor gets without choosing. Previous/next and one bullet per slide, all three injected by the module.

hero: cross-faded, rotating, controls over the image

Rotates every six seconds and stops the moment you rest the pointer on it or Tab into it — the two cheap halves of WCAG 2.2.2. The third is the stop/start control at the left of the bar, whose name changes rather than carrying aria-pressed: for a transport control the useful announcement is what pressing does next. class="m-slider--overlay" lays the bar over the image's bottom edge. If your system is set to reduce motion, this one starts stopped and the slide change is instant.

gallery: one, two or three per view

One slide per view on a phone, two from the small breakpoint and three from large — resize the window. The preset states those two steps by NAME, not in pixels, so it reads its numbers from the same _breakpoints.scss the stylesheets do. The distance between slides is --slider-gap, one declaration that feeds both the no-script strip and Swiper's spaceBetween.

One slide, and an unknown type

Two things that must not break. The first slider holds a single slide: no controls, no instance, no ready attribute — the markup is already exactly what it should look like, and an editor who filled a slider with one thing has not made a mistake. The second names a type that does not exist: the module reports it in the console and falls back to the default preset, because an editor's typo must not cost the slider.
